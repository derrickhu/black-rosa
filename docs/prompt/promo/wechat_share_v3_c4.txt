A wildly exaggerated 4:3 poster for a WeChat chat card. One joke, readable at thumbnail size. Flat cel-shaded sticker art, thick soy-sauce-brown outlines, hard edges, chunky shapes.

The character 炸 itself is the explosion. It is enormous, filling most of the frame, written in thick wet black ink. The strokes are bursting apart: the left side 火 and the right side 乍 are cracking open, and from the split pours a fountain of ancient Chinese cash coins and flames. The coins are round discs the color of a gold coin, each with a square hole and a blank metal face, spraying to the top edge like a jackpot. The flames are the color of a red envelope.

Tiny chibi ink people, charcoal bodies with two plain white oval eyes, are riding the coin spray, arms up, small against the giant character.

At the very bottom, one toy-sized wooden cannon on a rope-bound cart points straight up, its muzzle a small red-envelope flame touching the bottom stroke of 炸, like a fuse. The ground is a thin strip of beach sand. The sky behind the burst is clear midday blue.

The giant splitting character and the coin fountain are the first thing a person sees. The cannon is only the punchline at the bottom.
