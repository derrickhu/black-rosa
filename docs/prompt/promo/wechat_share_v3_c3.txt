A wildly exaggerated 4:3 poster for a WeChat chat card. One joke, readable at thumbnail size. Flat cel-shaded sticker art, thick soy-sauce-brown outlines, hard edges, chunky shapes.

A cream paper tile the size of a dam fills the frame, the color of steamed-bun dough, rounded corners, a thick soy-sauce outline. The black character 火 is written huge across it, and the tile is splitting down the middle like a cracking wall. Through the crack, a flood of wet calligraphy ink pours forward, the color of fresh black ink. Inside the flood are dozens of chibi ink people, charcoal bodies, two plain white oval eyes, packed so tight they read as one black wave about to burst into the foreground.

At the bottom center, one tiny wooden cannon on a rope-bound cart, small as a toy compared with the dam, barrel pointed up at the crack, muzzle flashing a flame the color of a red envelope. The scale gap is the whole picture: the cannon is a speck, the cracking tile and the ink flood are enormous.

A few ancient Chinese cash coins tumble in the splash: round discs the color of a gold coin, a square hole in the center, a blank metal face. Bright flat daylight.
